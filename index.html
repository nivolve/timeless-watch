Anatomy of a movement

Four hundred and seven parts.Not one of them hidden.

Every bridge is beveled by hand until its edges catch light like a knife’s. Every screw head is black-polished on a tin lap. Every jewel is set under a loupe, by the same watchmaker who will sign the finished piece.

The atelier

Craft, notcompromise.

Most skeleton movements pass through a dozen hands before they reach a case. Ours does not. Every Timeless Luxe calibre is drawn, assembled, regulated, and cased by a single watchmaker, working alone in a single atelier in the Vallée de Joux — the same hands from the first bridge to the final wind.

The twin balance wheels are not a flourish. Coupled through a differential and set beating in opposition, they cancel out the small positional errors a single oscillator cannot — the kind of correction usually reserved for a maison’s most serious chronometers, made visible here because there is nothing left to hide it behind.

Each bridge is hand-beveled under a loupe until its edges catch light like a knife’s. Screw heads are black-polished by hand on a tin lap, a technique that rewards patience and punishes shortcuts in equal measure. The finishing takes longer than the assembly. It always does.

A watch built this way cannot be rushed, and we have stopped trying. Each piece carries the mark of the watchmaker who made it. One name. One movement. One owner.
